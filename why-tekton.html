Deforestation

A forest stays standing
every time we ship a pole.

The United States installs or replaces roughly 4 million utility poles every year. Each one of those poles — historically — required a harvested tree. Old growth timber, managed forest, or otherwise: the tree was gone.

Tekton poles require zero harvested trees. The proprietary composite material system is manufactured from non-timber inputs, produced in a controlled facility, and deployed directly to field crews. The forest never has to pay for it.

At Tekton's production capacity, the math becomes extraordinary. Replacing even a fraction of America's wooden pole inventory with Tekton means millions of trees that stand, breathe, and do their job.

Chemical-Free Manufacturing

Wood poles are treated with toxic preservatives — creosote, pentachlorophenol (penta), and chromated copper arsenate (CCA) — to resist decay. These chemicals leach into soil and groundwater throughout the pole's service life and create hazardous waste disposal requirements at end of life.

Creosote
Carcinogenic PAHs. Classified as a probable human carcinogen. Contaminates soil for decades post-removal.
Pentachlorophenol (Penta)
EPA-listed toxic substance. Persists in soil and water. Bioaccumulates in wildlife. Restricted under multiple environmental regulations.
Tekton
Zero chemical preservatives. No contamination risk. No hazardous disposal. Clean from first day to last.
Chemical Contamination

Zero chemicals.
Zero contamination.
Zero compromise.

Traditional wooden utility poles are chemical delivery systems. The preservatives that keep them standing for 30 years don't disappear — they migrate into the soil and groundwater around every pole, every year, for decades.

Tekton poles contain no chemical preservatives. No treatment. No leaching. No remediation cost at end of life. The ground around every Tekton pole stays clean from installation day to decommission day.

For municipalities, water districts, and communities near utility corridors, this isn't a minor improvement. It's a fundamental change in what infrastructure does to the land it stands on.

Carbon Footprint

The lowest carbon
footprint in the
utility pole market.

Utility poles are everywhere — and most people don't think about the carbon cost of producing them. Steel poles generate nearly 4.5x the CO₂ of Tekton poles per thousand units. Concrete is even worse. Wood falls in between — but still produces more than twice the carbon output of Tekton.

At scale — replacing millions of poles — the carbon math becomes one of the most significant infrastructure-related emissions reductions available in the utility sector today.

Zero water consumption in manufacturing further reduces the environmental cost of production. Tekton is not just the better pole — it's the responsible one.
